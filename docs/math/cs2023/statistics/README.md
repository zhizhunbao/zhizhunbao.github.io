# 3. Statistics: term checklist

Not built (README). Source: *Online Statistics Education: An Interactive Multimedia Course of Study* (onlinestatbook.com/2, Rice University, University of Houston Clear Lake and Tufts University; public domain), cached 2026-10-29: chapter and section pages in `design-refs/official-docs/cache/data/onlinestatbook-raw/`, the glossary (195 entries, one page each) in `cache/md/onlinestatbook-2-glossary-*`. Introduction to Modern Statistics was the source until 2026-10-29; its end-of-chapter terms tables are teaching vocabulary, its section titles are lessons, and its definitions are running sentences, so it was replaced. OpenStax *Introductory Statistics 2e* was compared too (Key Terms uneven: 41 in chapter 1, 1 each in the chi-square and regression chapters).

The terms are the book's glossary. Each glossary term is placed in the section whose title names it (or one of the synonyms the glossary page lists), else in the section whose text names it most often; terms placed by hand are marked. The quote is the first paragraph of the glossary page. Sections that are demos, simulations, exercises or Statistical Literacy pages are not counted, so section numbers count the remaining sections in the book's order. Six glossary entries are words from the book's examples, not statistics, and are left out: Beta Carotene, Draft Number, iMac, Lies, M and Ms, Touchdown Pass.

The glossary has no entry for the topics of chapters 8 and 16 to 19 (and few for 17 to 19); there the terms are the topics the sections name, quoted from the sentence of the section that names them. Left out for lack of a defining sentence: Tukey's ladder of powers (16.2), goodness of fit (17.2), expected frequency (17.2, 17.3), distribution-free test (18.1), number needed to treat (19.1).

What the book lacks is added as chapters 20 to 23: bootstrapping, logistic regression and cross-validation from Çetinkaya-Rundel and Hardin, *Introduction to Modern Statistics* (2e), rows kept from the earlier IMS pages; Bayesian statistics from Poldrack, *Statistical Thinking for the 21st Century*, chapter 11, whose terms are the words it italicizes there.
