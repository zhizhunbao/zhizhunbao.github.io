# The Midterm Marked Zero

A multiple-choice midterm that I wrote in pencil **came back as 0**, so I wrote to the professor, kept a record of every step, read the college's grade review rules, and **made a plan with a date for each step**.

## What Happened

In one of my courses this term, the midterm was **100 multiple-choice questions** on an answer sheet that a machine scans. I filled in the sheet **with a pencil**, and **the professor looked at my sheet during the exam**. Classmates said afterwards that the exam was close to the weekly quizzes.

About two weeks after the exam, the professor mentioned in class that **three students had failed**. On a Saturday night, more than three weeks after the exam, the grade appeared online: mine was **0 out of 10, with no feedback**. **Three of us had exactly 0.**

With four choices per question, even random guessing gives **about 25 out of 100**. So I don't think the 0 is the mark I earned; I think my answer sheet was **not scanned, was not matched to my student number, or was entered wrongly**.

## What I Did

| # | Date | What I did |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2026‑11‑14 | That same night I **emailed the professor**: I wrote the exam, the mark shows 0, and could he check. |
| 2 | 2026‑11‑16 | I had **no reply**, so I answered on the same thread, said that I had used a pencil and that he had seen my sheet, and asked to **look at the answer sheet together** on Tuesday. |
| 3 | 2026‑11‑16 | I saved **full-screen screenshots** of my grades and of my submitted labs, with the date and time on the screen, and wrote a **timeline** of what happened on which day. |

## My Plan

1. Tuesday, before class: **ask the professor in person**. I will say that I need his help, that I think my answer sheet was not scanned correctly, and ask **when I can expect the result**.

2. Tuesday evening: send a short email that **confirms what he said**.

3. Friday, the **fifth business day**: if there is no result, ask the **Office of the Ombudsman** for advice, and ask **the chair** to help.

4. Only after that: a **formal grade review**, and an **appeal** if it is needed.

## Reflections

- **I check a grade the day it comes out.** A review has a deadline, and I was inside it only because I looked that night.

- **I write first, talk second, and write again after the talk**, so that everything said has a date.

- **I state facts and ask for help.** I say "I need your help, I think my sheet was not scanned", not "you made a mistake".

- **I read the rules before I decide what to do.** Once I knew the deadlines and whom to ask next, I was much less anxious.

- **I count the cost of everything.** Someone else's small mistake cost me days of time and sleep, so I try to settle it at the cheapest step and move to a more expensive step only when I have to.
