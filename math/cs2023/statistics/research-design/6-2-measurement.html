              Reliability
              信度              		 "Although there are many ways to conceive of the reliability of a test, the classical way is to define the reliability as the correlation between two parallel forms of the test. When defined this way, the reliability is the ratio of true score variance to test score variance. Chronbach's \(\alpha\) is a common measure of reliability." 尽管有许多方式来设想一项测验的信度，但经典的方式是将信度定义为测验两个平行形式之间的相关。这样定义时，信度是真分数方差与测验分数方差之比。Chronbach 的 \(\alpha\) 是常用的信度度量。 Online Statistics Education, n.d., glossary

              True Score
              真分数              		 "In classical test theory, the true score is a theoretical value that represents a test taker's score without error. If a person took parallel forms of a test thousands of times (assuming no practice or fatigue effects), the mean of all their scores would be a good approximation of their true score since the error would be almost entirely averaged out. It should be distinguished from validity." 在经典测验理论中，真分数是代表应试者无误差得分的理论值。如果一个人把平行形式的测验做了数千次（假设没有练习或疲劳效应），则其所有得分的均值将是其真分数的良好近似，因为误差几乎被完全平均掉了。应将其与效度区分开来。 Online Statistics Education, n.d., glossary
