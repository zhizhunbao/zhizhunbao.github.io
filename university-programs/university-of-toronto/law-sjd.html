              Courses

              Level Type Course Code Course Name Credits Source
              Coursework. Students must successfully complete the mandatory graduate seminars (0.75 full-course equivalent [FCE]) as follows: University of Toronto, n.d.
              LAW1007H SJD Seminar — Year 1 0.75 University of Toronto, n.d.
              LAW1008H SJD Seminar — Year 2 0.00 University of Toronto, n.d.
              LAW1009H SJD Seminar — Year 3 0.00 University of Toronto, n.d.
              Other coursework requirements are optional and shall be determined upon consultation with the supervisor. All coursework shall be subject to the approval of the Associate Dean, Graduate Studies, Law. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Area Exam. Before being allowed to proceed with formal research on a thesis topic, a student must demonstrate competence in a broader area within which the topic falls. The student's supervisory committee (established by the student and approved by the Associate Dean of Graduate Studies at the Faculty of Law) assists in framing that area and compiling an appropriate plan for carrying out the research. The research undertaken by the student culminates in a research project, which is either a draft of a chapter of the thesis or an overview of the general argument on which the student is examined orally. Unless approved by the Associate Dean of Graduate Studies, a student must satisfy the area requirement by the end of Year 1 of registration. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Consequence of failure: Failure to undertake and pass the Area Exam at the end of Year 1 (session 3) constitutes the basis for termination of registration in the SJD program. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Year 2 presentation. At the end of Year 2 of registration, students must present an abstract of their work in progress or a draft chapter to an audience of their peers and interested faculty. The purpose of the meeting is to provide the student with a forum to collect feedback from a broad audience. Unless approved by the Associate Dean of Graduate Studies, a student must satisfy the Year 2 presentation by the end of Year 2 (session 6) of registration. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Consequence of failure: Failure to deliver and pass the Year 2 presentation constitutes the basis for termination of registration in the SJD program. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Research and writing. A student will not be allowed to continue in the doctoral program, where, in the opinion of the Supervisory Committee, the student is not capable of demonstrating the capacity for independent legal research and writing at an advanced level. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Annual meetings. Students must meet with their entire supervisory committee at least once a year. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Thesis. Following completion of the requirements above, a thesis must be prepared which, in the opinion of the Faculty of Law, constitutes a distinct contribution to legal research or scholarship, and the student must pass a Doctoral Final Oral Examination based on the thesis. University of Toronto, n.d.
              The thesis must be completed within five years from the date of enrolment in the program to reflect program time limits. University of Toronto, n.d.
              No candidate will be recommended for the degree until the thesis has been approved by the Faculty of Law and is presented in publishable form, as described in the PhD regulations in this calendar. University of Toronto, n.d.
              Residence. Students must be in full-time attendance for at least six academic sessions (equivalent to three academic years): University of Toronto, n.d.
              September to December (Fall session) and January to April (Winter session) for the first three years of enrolment in the program (F/W, F/W, FW). University of Toronto, n.d.
              In limited circumstances, including personal emergencies and engagement in approved academic activities outside of the University, students will be permitted to request a temporary, short-term exemption from the residency requirement and obligation to attend the SJD seminar courses for three consecutive years. Such requests shall be made of the Associate Dean, Graduate Programs. University of Toronto, n.d.
